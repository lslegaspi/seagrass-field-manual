Field manual: gastropods in seagrass

Mactan Island, Cebu. Marigondon Beach and Day-As Port Baywalk.

Before you go

Check the tide table. Sampling happens only at daytime low spring tide, ideally early morning. Never go alone. Skip the trip after heavy rain.

Gear checklist

    Team check

      Lay out the site

      Each site: 3 transects, each 100 m long, running straight out from the shore, 25 m apart. A quadrat goes at every 10 m mark: 11 per transect, 33 per site.

      1. Find the shoreline and pick the start of Transect 1. Record the GPS point.
      2. Run the tape from 0 m (shore) to 100 m (seaward), perpendicular to the shore.
      3. Measure 25 m along the shore and lay Transects 2 and 3 parallel to the first.
      4. Round 1 only: mark every transect start with a stake and its GPS point. Round 2 must use the exact same lines.
      5. Walk beside the line, never on it, so you don’t trample the quadrats.

      Tap each quadrat when finished

      Shoreline (0 m)
      Seaward (100 m)
      0 / 33 quadrats

      Quadrat squares are not to scale. Progress saves on this phone only.

      At each quadrat

      Use the 1 × 1 m quadrat. Do the steps in this order, so nothing is disturbed before it is recorded.

      1. Place the frame at the mark. Write down the transect, quadrat number and distance from shore.
      2. Seagrass cover: lay the 5 × 5 grid frame. Each grid intersection counts as 4%. Estimate cover for each seagrass species separately.
      3. Two observers estimate cover independently, then you average the two numbers.
      4. Identify the seagrass species using the BFAR Field Guide on Seagrass (see Prep tab) for accurate species coding. Then note the dominant species and the substrate: sand, muddy sand or rubble.
      5. Photograph the quadrat before touching anything.
      6. Gastropod census: find every gastropod in the frame, whatever its species, size or position.
      7. For each one, photograph it where it sits, then record shell length (Vernier calipers) and where it was found: seagrass leaf, rhizome or sediment surface, or bare substrate.
      8. Identify it to the lowest level you can using the Guide to Marine Gastropods of the Philippines (see Prep tab). If you can’t, name it Morphospecies 1, 2, 3 and keep using the same number all study.
      9. Put non-voucher animals back where you found them.

      Water quality (once per site per round)

      Use the multiparameter probe at mid-water depth during sampling. Wear the wrist lanyard. Record temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, EC, TDS, salinity, SSG, BGA-PC and ORP. Rinse the probe with freshwater afterwards.

      Datasheet columns

      Round, site, transect, quadratplus date, tide and observers
      Seagrass% cover per species (two observers), dominant species, substrate
      Each gastropodID or morphospecies, shell length (mm), where found, photo number

      Samples and photos

      Identification

      Use shell shape, aperture, ribbing and color. Use the Guide to Marine Gastropods of the Philippines (link on the Prep tab). Photo every animal first, because photos are the audit record for every identification.

      Voucher shells

      1. Collect only 1 to 3 individuals per morphospecies per site. Nothing else is taken.
      2. Rinse with distilled water and air dry on absorbent paper in shade. Do not use ethanol or fluids.
      3. Store each shell in its own labeled vial or resealable bag.
      4. Label with acid-free paper: site, transect, quadrat, date, collector, shell length.
      5. In the lab, check under the microscope. If the lab ID differs from the field ID, the lab ID wins.

      Never collect seagrass, coral or substrate. Don’t disturb anything outside your quadrats.

      Waste

      Pack out everything: gloves, bags, food wrappers, paper. Nothing stays at the site. Rinse water goes down the sink. Segregate trash by LGU rules.

      Stay safe

      Sun and heatGo at early low tide. Wear a rashguard, hat and reef-safe sunscreen. Bring water and electrolytes.
      Slips and cutsWear boots or amphibious shoes with grip. Move slowly. Watch for sharp shells, coral bits and debris.
      Handling shellsWear gloves. Mind sharp edges and opercula.
      Dirty waterGloves and boots on. Don’t put hands or face in the water. Wash with soap right after. Skip fieldwork after heavy rain.
      TravelSeat belt on the road. Life vest on any boat. Don’t travel in rough weather.
      Buddy ruleNobody works alone. Keep the first aid kit at the site.
      GearKeep phones and cameras in waterproof cases. Rinse the probe after use.
